### [1] [A multi-scale immuno-epidemiological behavioral model for influenza-like illness: connecting scales through symptom scores](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00862)
*Binod Pant,Summer Atkins,Necibe Tuncer,Hana M. Dobrovolny*

Main category: q-bio.PE

TL;DR: 本文提出一个多尺度模型，用系统性症状评分（而非病毒载量）驱动有症状个体的行为改变，发现更强的疾病驱动行为会推迟并降低流行高峰，且其轨迹与标准SEIR模型定性相似。

Motivation: 许多传染病中，行为改变并非人群层面对病例数上升的反应，而是个体层面由自身病感触发的个人反应。然而大多数模型未能刻画疾病严重程度如何驱动这一反应。现有多尺度模型多以病毒载量为桥梁连接免疫尺度与流行病学尺度，但病毒载量常与症状严重程度不一致，因而可能不适合用于指导人群层面的行为。作者因此希望构建一个以症状严重程度驱动行为改变的多尺度模型。

Method: 作者建立一个新颖的多尺度模型：有症状个体根据系统性症状评分（由肌肉酸痛、疲劳、头痛和发热感构成的综合指标，并由宿主内免疫动态所决定）动态调整其传播率，而无症状个体不改变行为。模型在连接两个尺度的多种不同连接函数下进行考察，并与行为改变由人群层面反馈（如报告病例或死亡数）驱动的模型进行对比。

Result: 更强的疾病驱动行为改变会推迟并降低流行高峰。此外，在多种连接函数选择下，由此产生的流行轨迹与不含行为改变的标准SEIR模型在定性上仍然相似。这与由人群层面反馈驱动的行为改变模型形成对比——后者可产生定性上不同的特征，如平台期、肩峰或延长的流行下降期。当使用症状评分的指数函数作为连接函数时，标准SEIR模型能够很好地复现多尺度模型在给定时间点产生的总感染人数。

Conclusion: 以症状严重程度（而非病毒载量）驱动行为改变的多尺度建模更能反映个体层面的真实反应；其流行轨迹在定性上与标准SEIR模型相似，且在特定连接函数下可由SEIR模型近似复现总感染人数。这为将个体病感纳入流行病建模提供了新思路，并区别于人群反馈驱动的行为模型。

Abstract: For many infectious diseases, behavior change is not a population-level reaction to rising case counts, but a personal one, triggered by how sick an individual feels. Yet most models fail to capture how illness severity drives this response. Multi-scale models that link the immunological scale to the epidemiological scale seem well-suited for capturing this behavior. Most multi-scale models link within-host viral load to population-level transmission rates through linking functions that are a function of viral load. However, viral load is often misaligned with symptom severity and thus possibly ill-suited for the purpose of informing behavior at a population level. We present a novel multi-scale model in which symptomatic individuals dynamically modify their transmission based on systemic symptom scores (a composite measure of muscle ache, fatigue, headache, and feverishness informed by within-host immune dynamics) while asymptomatic individuals do not change behavior. We find that stronger illness-driven behavior change delays and lowers the epidemic peak. Moreover, the resulting epidemic trajectory remains qualitatively similar to that of a standard SEIR model without behavior change, across several distinct choices of linking functions connecting the two scales. This stands in contrast to models where behavior change is driven by population-level feedback (e.g., reported cases or deaths), which can produce qualitatively distinct signatures such as plateaus, shoulders, or an elongated epidemic decline. In the case when an exponential function of symptom score is used as a linking function, we show that the standard SEIR model can closely reproduce the total number of infectious individuals at a given point in time generated through the multi-scale level.
